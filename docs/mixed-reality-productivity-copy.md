## Case-study body

This project explored how Meta VR Glasses could support everyday work. I led the early design concepts for the productivity vision at Reality Labs, working with designers and partners across product, research, and engineering. The designs below show team concepts, not shipped screens.

### The project

As the device took shape, teams were exploring different parts of productivity. We needed a shared picture of the person using it and the tasks it should support. That meant looking beyond individual features to the transitions between browsing, messaging, writing and computer work.

The early vision work used everyday scenarios to make those transitions visible. Each scenario helped the teams discuss where a private spatial workspace could be useful, what should stay familiar and which parts of the experience depended on one another.

### Work journey

One concept follows a person through a planning task. Browser, calendar and chat are visible together so a message or appointment can inform what happens next. The scenario then moves into a document and a comparison between options. It gave the team a concrete way to examine how context carries across apps.

The concept uses the room as a workspace, while keeping each window tied to the task. A person can refer to two sources beside a working document rather than repeatedly replacing one view with another.

### Inputs

The work journey also needed a way to write. We explored a virtual keyboard and touchpad on a flat surface, with the document and conversation still in view. The concept connects a familiar action, typing at a table, to the spatial workspace around it.

### Laptop extension

Another part of the vision considered work people already had on a computer. The remote-display concepts explored reconnecting to a previous layout and choosing a screen format suited to the task. Browser, files and calendar explorations looked at the same broader question: how can someone pick up work without rebuilding their workspace each time?

### Calls alongside the work

An early exploration placed a video call and screen sharing alongside a document and browser. A person could discuss a choice while keeping the material in view. This was a concept for the productivity vision, not a shipped interface.

At Meta Connect 2026, Meta described hands-free hologram calling with spatial audio. People joining on a phone or computer see the wearer’s hologram on their screen, and Meta named WhatsApp and Zoom among supported apps. The official image below shows a call on the announced device.

### The result

The work brought the separate concepts into an end-to-end productivity reference for more than ten contributing teams. The scenarios informed one of three program-wide personas and gave product, design and engineering teams a common basis for scoping, beta scenarios and weekly playtests.

The result was a clearer picture of what everyday work on the device could involve. These images document early design thinking; they do not imply that each interaction became a final feature.
